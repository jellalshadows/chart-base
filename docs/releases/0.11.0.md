# 0.11.0: execution record

This record keeps what happened while 0.11.0 was designed, built and reviewed: the scope cut that defined it, the
process, the decisions and the rulings with their reasons, the deviations from the plan, every review finding and what
became of it, and the measurements that changed a decision. It is written for a reader who never saw the working files
of the execution; those files are named here by their role (the design, the design critique, the plan, the prototype
report, the prototype's measurement logs, the execution report, the final review and the fix wave's report), not by
their location.

Roles: the **owner** of the repository authorized the execution on 2026-10-03 to run without asking, merges and
releases included (the autonomous mode); the **controller** ruled on every question and finding and dispatched every
step; the **designer** wrote and revised the design; the **critic** critiqued it; the **prototyper** built the
prototype and the plan; the **implementer** applied the plan's tasks; the **reviewer** checked the whole branch; the
**fix wave** applied the review's findings and wrote this record. The designer, the critic, the prototyper, the
reviewer and the fix wave ran on the judgment model (Opus); the implementer ran on Sonnet.

## What 0.11.0 is

The last release before 1.0. `commonLabels` join every labels map the chart writes (every object's metadata, the pod
templates, the CronJob's job template, an `ephemeral` claim template and the ExternalSecret's
`template.metadata.labels`), never a selector; `commonAnnotations` join every object's metadata, never a template's. A
per-object map wins on a shared key, an empty value included, and the keys the chart or Helm writes fail the schema.
`service.annotations` annotate the Service. `extraObjects` renders objects the chart does not offer: a map keyed by the
object's key, each named `<fullname>.<key>` in the release namespace, a `null` entry or field absent at any depth
through maps, rendered as written; the built-in workloads, `Service`, `Endpoints`, `EndpointSlice`, `Secret`, lists, a
RoleBinding named like the chart's and the cluster-scoped built-in kinds fail the render with a remedy; on a `job`
component every extra object is a hook of the Job's phase
([ADR-0057](../adr/0057-common-labels-and-annotations-service-annotations-and-extraobjects.md),
[upgrade notes](../upgrading.md#010x--0110)).

## The scope cut and the process

### The owner's scope cut (2026-11-02)

Three more releases were planned before 1.0: an earlier 0.11.0 (GRPCRoute, HTTPRoute rules and `appProtocol`, with a
stricter `httpRoute` validation that made it breaking), a 0.12.0 (common metadata, `extraObjects` and a chart-wide
`null` rule) and a 0.13.0 (a StatefulSet). The earlier 0.11.0 had been designed, critiqued, revised and half
prototyped; its prototyper was stopped on 2026-10-18 at the owner's pause. On 2026-11-02 the owner asked for what any
company needs instead of an enormous chart, and after the controller's proposal approved the cut:

- **Cut**, as post-1.0 candidates for when a real umbrella asks: the earlier 0.11.0, the chart-wide `null` rule, the
  StatefulSet, and reserved keys for the existing maps (`podLabels`, `podAnnotations`, `ingress.annotations`,
  `serviceAccount.annotations`). Those checks would reject values that render today, so they join the 1.0 freeze's list
  of breaking checks.
- **Kept**, as this 0.11.0, `feat:`: `commonLabels`, `commonAnnotations`, `service.annotations` and `extraObjects`,
  with the earlier 0.12.0's rulings on extra objects (a name prefixed with `<fullname>`, no foreign namespace, a
  denylist of kinds, a `job` component's extra objects as hooks of its phase). Then 1.0.0: validation with a real
  umbrella, and the contract freeze.

The same day, the working files of the cut releases (their designs, research and critiques, the half-done prototype),
the archived execution ledgers and the pinned Helm and shellcheck binaries were found deleted from the controller's
scratch directory under the system's temporary directory (cause not verified, most likely a temporary-file cleanup).
The repository, the plans and the rulings were safe, and the earlier records keep the ledgers' content. Ruling: every
agent works in a working directory outside the temporary directory and outside the repository, and the pinned binaries
live next to it. Cost if wrong: disk space.

### The process change (2026-11-03)

The new 0.11.0 was planned with the full method: design, one critique, revision, prototype and plan, an independent
plan review, then per task an implementer, the replay checker and a reviewer, two final lenses and one fix wave. On
2026-11-03 the owner asked how to cut the time. The controller measured that the whole suite takes about 26 s on the
owner's machine, so the time goes to the agents' review gates, not to the tools. The owner first chose a lighter
process for 1.0 only, then widened it the same day: do everything that remains straight through, then test everything
needed in Docker at the end of the project and fix what that finds, since otherwise the project would never finish.
Applied to 0.11.0:

- **No independent plan review.** The prototype had already been verified by a replay of the plan, a round trip of its
  Markdown and the checker.
- **One implementer** ran Tasks 1-5 in one pass, with the replay checker after each task and no task review.
- **One final whole-branch review and one fix wave**, without a second lens or a scoped re-review. The controller's
  ruling: with the plan review and the task reviews gone, it is the only independent look before a version is
  published. Cost if wrong: about 35 minutes per release; skipping it would publish defects that only the final
  campaign finds, as patch releases.
- **The pull request's CI**, both e2e legs included, still gates the merge.
- **The final net:** before 1.0.0 is cut, an exhaustive validation campaign runs in Docker with no time cap, and what it
  finds is fixed. Candidates: every `ci/` scenario and README recipe installed on real clusters at both Kubernetes edges
  and with both Helm versions; a real two-alias umbrella installed, upgraded and rolled back; upgrades into the
  candidate from the published releases. The 1.0 preparation itself runs design, implementation and review.

### What the first replay caught, and why

The first full replay of the plan ended Task 5 with 5 differences from the prototype's state
(`.github/scripts/alias-contract.sh`, `.github/scripts/e2e.sh`, `ci/full-values.yaml`, `tests/snapshot_test.yaml` and
the snapshot file). It had two causes:

- **A plan defect.** Task 4's alias-mutation step reused the snapshot guard of Tasks 1-3, which restores `main`'s
  snapshot when a run changed it. In Task 4 the snapshot is legitimately changed (Step 3 regenerates it, not yet
  committed), so the guard undid the regeneration. Task 4's commit block then refused (its snapshot must differ from
  `main`), and its four other files stayed uncommitted. A real executor would have stopped at the same refusal. Fixed in
  the plan's source: that step checks instead that the snapshot still differs from `main`, and the replay asserts it.
- **A tooling defect in the measured expectations.** Two measuring runs overlapped and shared one script file, so one
  recorded line carried the other run's output. Fixed with one script file per process, and Task 4's expectations were
  measured again.

The second replay ran with 0 problems and 0 differences at every state. One cosmetic defect it showed was fixed and
re-run: a lychee filter printed lychee's trailing blank line instead of its summary.

## The release type: `feat:`, not breaking

- **Every key 0.11.0 adds was a schema error on `main`** (the root and `service` are closed). None is required, every
  reader takes a missing key or `null` as empty, and the defaults (`{}`) render nothing. Every new rule reads only a
  new key, so no value that `main` accepts is rejected.
- **Byte identity, measured three times** (stdout, stderr and exit code of `helm template`, Helm 4.3.0 and 3.22.0):
  - the prototype: 88 of 88, covering the 7 `ci/` scenarios, the 7 shared test values, the 23 values blocks of the
    README's recipes (each through an umbrella of its keys, the overlay over the block before it) and the 7 scenarios
    with `main`'s `values.yaml` as the defaults (the `--reuse-values` shape);
  - the final review: 48 of 48 on the last task's commit (its own set, with the empty defaults and three extra shapes
    of existing keys);
  - the fix wave, on its last code commit: the prototype's 88 cases, 88 of 88; the review's three extra shapes, 6 of 6.
    The empty defaults fail the schema on both charts with the same lines, in an order that varies from run to run for
    each chart alone.
- **`--reuse-values`, measured with real upgrades:** the prototype upgraded releases installed from the published 0.10.0
  (digest `sha256:30a58cf0c23ba7b92a2271b33e647ead4014acdc9ca3098884b0777870ab4bd8` checked) into the prototype
  versioned 0.11.0, on kube-apiserver 1.37.0 with Helm 4.3.0 and 3.22.0. There were six direct installs (every scenario
  but `job`, whose hook Job waits for a Job controller) and an umbrella of two aliases. All 14 upgrades exited 0, and
  revision 2's manifest was revision 1's but for the `helm.sh/chart` label. Skipped versions: 0.9.0's and 0.10.0's
  values render; 0.4.0's to 0.8.0's fail with the lines of 0.9.0's upgrade notes.
- **The published 0.11.0 changes the `helm.sh/chart` label** in every object's metadata, never in a pod template
  ([ADR-0010](../adr/0010-chart-version-never-restarts-pods.md)). The documents say "but for the `helm.sh/chart` label"
  (F3).

## Measurements that changed or confirmed a decision

- **The lint claim** (the critique, B2): a `propertyNames` rule and an unknown key fail `helm lint --strict` directly
  and through an umbrella, on both Helm versions; a guard never does. The documents state it per rule, and the fix wave
  corrected the one sentence that read the guards into it (F2).
- **A hook key turns an object into a hook** (the critique, B3, and the prototype): the design's first log of this
  showed `unknown flag: --kube-version`. Re-measured, `ingress.annotations: {helm.sh/hook: pre-install}` on `main`'s
  chart installs with exit 0 on kube-apiserver 1.37.0 with both Helm versions. `helm get hooks` lists the Ingress,
  `helm get manifest` lists the other objects, and the Ingress stays after `helm uninstall`. This is why the new maps
  reject Helm's hook keys.
- **Names with a `.`** (the prototype, `kubectl create --dry-run=server` on kube-apiserver 1.33.0 and 1.37.0): accepted
  for 13 kinds (a ConfigMap, ServiceAccount, Role, RoleBinding, NetworkPolicy, PersistentVolumeClaim,
  PodDisruptionBudget, HorizontalPodAutoscaler, Ingress, Lease, LimitRange, ResourceQuota and an ExternalSecret named
  `vending-api.x`), and for a Pod whose `configMap` volume and `envFrom` name one. A Service so named was rejected on
  both servers, which is O1's reason.
- **The cluster-scoped kinds:** `CustomResourceDefinition` and `APIService` are served cluster-scoped on both servers;
  this pair was the design's UNVERIFIED entry. No built-in cluster-scoped kind is served outside the table, measured by
  the prototype with the CRDs it installed and by the final review with every API enabled (36 kinds on 1.33.0, 39 on
  1.37.0). The table keeps the kinds those servers do not serve by default (its mechanical rule).
- **The bytes of a hook object** (judgment call 1): `chart-base.supportHookAnnotations` renders a whitespace-only line
  under `annotations:`. The annotations helper therefore takes the chart's own annotations as text, so every hook object
  keeps `main`'s bytes.
- **A mutation that survived the design's tests** (judgment call 10): without a pass test for a RoleBinding of
  `example.com/v1` keyed like a ClusterRole, the mutation that reads the collision from the kind alone survived.
- **`merge` fills an empty destination value** (the final review, F1): an empty per-object value rendered the common
  value. The fix wave sets each per-object key over a copy of the common map.
- **A YAML 1.1 boolean word as an `extraObjects` key** (the fix wave, R-Y, `helm template` on Helm 4.3.0 and 3.22.0):
  unquoted `y`, `n`, `yes`, `no`, `on` and `off` (lowercase, capitalized or uppercase) render `<fullname>.true` or
  `<fullname>.false`. The key guard then sees `true` or `false`, so `ON` passes although the guard rejects it as
  written. Two such keys make one object (the later entry wins) without an error, and a quoted key or a `--set` path
  keeps the word. Documented, with no rule added: no schema rule can see the key before YAML has read it.

## Controller decisions (the design)

1. **`feat:`, one pull request, released alone as 0.11.0**; the squash subject is the title with ` (#<PR>)` and the
   body is empty. Why: every key is new and was a schema error on `main`; byte identity and `--reuse-values` are
   measured; one CHANGELOG line.
2. **`commonLabels`** on every labels map the chart writes, the pod templates and an `ephemeral` claim template
   included, never on a selector; never `helm.sh/chart` or `managed-by` on a template; the seven chart keys rejected.
   Why: pod labels are what cost, logging and policy tools read (O2); one rule is simpler to state and test than a list
   of exceptions; selectors are immutable.
3. **`commonAnnotations`** on every object's metadata, never a template's. Rejected keys: `helm.sh/hook*`,
   `meta.helm.sh/*`, the two checksums and the Deployment's two Reloader keys. Why: pod annotations have readers of
   their own (`podAnnotations`); External Secrets executes the template's metadata as templates; a hook key turns an
   object into a hook.
4. **Precedence:** the chart's own keys first, in their order, then one sorted map in which the per-object value wins.
   On a `job`'s ServiceAccount, the hooks win over `serviceAccount.annotations`, which win over the common map. Why:
   `main`'s bytes stay while the new maps are empty.
5. **`service.annotations`:** strings, winning over the common map, with no guard when there is no Service. Why: a
   cloud load balancer reads them; a component without a Service renders nothing.
6. **The reserved keys are schema rules**, one definition per kind (`extraLabels`, `extraAnnotations`). Why: a schema
   rule fails `helm lint`; a guard only prints an INFO line. The cost: the message names the key at an unrelated path.
7. **`extraObjects`** is a map read through one accessor, with `null` absent at any depth through maps. Each object is
   named `<fullname>.<key>` (a guard checks the key), its `metadata` is closed to `labels` and `annotations`, and it is
   rendered as written (no `tpl`). Why:
   - an overlay changes or removes one entry;
   - `.` is the only alias-safe separator (`-` collides with a sibling alias, and the chart already names its
     ClusterRole bindings `<fullname>.<ClusterRole>`);
   - the chart stays the one writer of names ([ADR-0009](../adr/0009-no-name-overrides.md));
   - `tpl` would execute a consumer's string with the whole `.Values` reachable.
8. **52 rejected built-in kinds, lists and the RoleBinding collision**, each failing with a remedy, then `set
   extraObjects.<key>: null`. Below `apiVersion`, `kind` and `metadata` the object is an open pass-through; `Service` is
   rejected (O1); SealedSecret and SecretProviderClass pass (Q1). Why:
   - one workload and one Service per alias;
   - a Secret's data would sit in the values
     ([ADR-0004](../adr/0004-env-in-configmap-secrets-through-externalsecret.md));
   - a cluster-scoped kind has a cluster-wide name that collides across namespaces, and a namespace's deployer
     cannot own it;
   - Helm's client installs the items of any object with a top-level `items`;
   - an allowlist would refuse every namespaced kind the chart has not learnt.
9. **On a `job` component, extra objects are hooks of the Job's phase.** Why: they exist, with the new content, before
   the Job runs, like its support resources ([ADR-0007](../adr/0007-jobs-as-helm-hooks.md)).
10. **e2e:** only `full` changes, and every object of its release is read back from the cluster; `ci/job-values.yaml`
    is unchanged (A1). **Docs:** ADR-0057 amends ADR-0007, ADR-0010, ADR-0041, ADR-0049 and ADR-0053 by status line and
    index row.
11. **Autonomous mode:** Task 6 pushes, opens the pull request and watches CI; the controller merges, releases and
    verifies the publish.

**F1 (important): an empty per-object value lost to the common map.**

- *The finding:* `merge` treats an empty (`""`) destination value as unset and fills it from the source. `podLabels`,
  `service.annotations`, `serviceAccount.annotations` and an extra object's own labels and annotations rendered the
